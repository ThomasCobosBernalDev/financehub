Unit 1 · Lesson 4 Moving money

Money moves in seconds. Mistakes move just as fast.

The story so far You have checking at Riverbend and savings at Peak. Now your new campus job wants your bank details for your $600 paycheck. Your roommate wants $15 for pizza. Rent is due next week. And Grandma just mailed you a $200 check. Each one moves money a different way.

What

Money moves on "rails": direct deposit and bank transfers, wires, apps like Zelle and Venmo, and checks. Each has its own speed, cost and safety.

Why

Pick the wrong rail and you pay fees you didn't need, wait days, or send money you can never get back.

How

Match the rail to the job: free and slow when you can wait, fast when you must, and never instant to someone you don't know.

Who

Payroll specialists set up direct deposit. Treasury analysts move company cash. Payments product managers build apps like Zelle.

What you're looking at: a sketch, not data. Many fast payments travel one way: once your $15 arrives, only the person who got it can send it back.

01 See it your style

Every way to move money trades off speed, cost and safety.

First, how a paycheck reaches your account. Then the main ways to send money, what each costs, how long it takes, and which ones you can't undo.

Direct deposit: your paycheck on autopilot

With direct deposit, your employer sends your pay straight into your account. To set it up, you give two numbers. The routing number is 9 digits that say which bank to send to. Everyone at your bank shares it. The account number says which account at that bank is yours. Both are printed along the bottom of a check and in your banking app. Direct deposit travels on the ACH network, short for Automated Clearing House, which moves money between banks in batches.

In this example
Direct deposit
Your job pays $600 every other Friday into your Riverbend checking. No check to cash, no trip to the bank.
Routing number
Riverbend's is 099100002 (made up), the same for every member.
Account number
Yours is 4400218765 (made up). It's what makes the deposit land in your account and not someone else's.
ACH
In 2024 the ACH network moved 33.6 billion payments worth $86.2 trillion, including most U.S. paychecks.

What you're looking at: a made-up check. The line of numbers along the bottom is what your employer's payroll form asks for: the routing number, then your account number, then the check's own number.

Five ways to send money

A business day is Monday to Friday, not counting holidays, and most transfers count only those. A regular ACH transfer between your own banks is free and takes 1 to 3 business days. A wire transfer arrives the same business day but usually costs $25 to $35 to send, and it's almost impossible to pull back. A peer-to-peer payment, or P2P, sends money to a person by phone number or email. Zelle moves it between bank accounts in minutes. Venmo and Cash App hold it in an app balance first, then charge an instant transfer fee if you want it in your bank right away. Last, you can deposit a paper check by photo, a mobile deposit, but the bank may place a hold before you can spend it all.

In this example
Business day
Start a $300 move from Peak to Riverbend on Friday morning and it lands by Tuesday at the latest: Friday, Monday, Tuesday. Start it Friday night, after the cutoff, and it begins Monday, landing by Wednesday.
Wire transfer
Sending your $650 rent by wire would cost about $30, for speed you don't need.
Peer-to-peer payment
Zelle moved more than $1 trillion in 3.6 billion payments in 2024. Your $15 pizza share arrives in minutes.
Instant transfer fee
Cashing out $400 of Venmo now costs 1.75% × $400 = $7.00. Waiting 1 to 3 business days costs $0.
Mobile deposit
Grandma's $200 check: federal rules say the first $275 of a check deposit must usually be available the next business day, so all of it is.

What you're looking at: a calculator with each rail's typical speed and cost: ACH 1 to 3 business days for $0, a wire the same business day for $30, Zelle in minutes for $0, Venmo's standard transfer to your bank in 1 to 3 business days for $0, and Venmo's instant transfer for 1.75%, at least $0.25 and at most $25. "Latest" counts business days only.

Some payments can't be undone

With a debit or credit card, you can dispute a charge if something goes wrong, and the bank can pull the money back. Zelle, wires and friends-and-family app payments work like handing over cash: once they arrive, they're the other person's. That's why scammers ask for them. Payment apps sometimes offer purchase protection, but only on payments marked as goods and services. And app balances aren't bank deposits, so, as you learned when opening an account, they may not be FDIC insured.

In this example
Purchase protection
A stranger online sells you $120 concert tickets and asks for Zelle. If the tickets never come, there's no one to refund you. Paying by credit card, or from the official ticket seller, keeps protection on your side.

04 Break it down your style

The logic, step by step.

Four calculations tell you when money arrives and what speed costs.

Step 1: Count business days

Skip weekends and holidays. A transfer sent after the bank's cutoff, often late afternoon, starts the next business day.

Latest arrival = the 3rd business day, counting the day it starts
In this example
Friday night
Starts Monday: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday. Lands by Wednesday at the latest.
Friday morning
Starts Friday: Friday, Monday, Tuesday. Lands by Tuesday at the latest.

Step 2: The instant fee

Multiply the amount by 1.75%, then keep it between the $0.25 minimum and the $25 maximum.

Instant fee = amount × 0.0175, at least $0.25, at most $25
In this example
$400
$400 × 0.0175 = $7.00.
$2,000
$2,000 × 0.0175 = $35, which is over the cap, so $25.
$10
$10 × 0.0175 = $0.18, under the minimum, so $0.25.

Step 3: The fee as a share of what you move

Divide the fee by the amount to see what fraction speed is costing you.

Share lost = feeamount
In this example
A $30 wire for $650 rent
$30$650 = 0.046, about 4.6% of your rent.
Venmo instant
Always $7$400 = 1.75% until the cap.

Step 4: A check hold

For most check deposits, the first $275 is available the next business day and the rest usually by the second business day. Banks can hold longer for new accounts or deposits over $6,725.

Next day = the smaller of the check and $275  ·  Rest = check − next-day amount
In this example
$1,000 check on Monday
$275 on Tuesday, and the other $1,000 − $275 = $725 usually by Wednesday.

Worked example: a month of moving money

  1. Paycheck. Direct deposit with Riverbend's routing number and your account number: $600 every other Friday, $0.
  2. Pizza. $15 to your roommate by Zelle: minutes, $0, and you know them.
  3. Rent. $650 through your landlord's portal by ACH, started 3 business days early: $0. A wire would have cost about $30.
  4. Venmo balance. $400 cashed out by standard transfer: $0, instead of $7.00 instant.
  5. Grandma's check. $200 by mobile deposit: all of it the next business day, since it's under $275.
  6. Total spent on moving money: $0. Rushing every one of these could have cost $37 or more.
05 Nature link your style

Honeybees check twice before the swarm moves.

When a honeybee colony splits, thousands of bees hang in a cluster while scouts search for a new home. A scout that finds a good hollow dances to recruit others, and those bees fly out to inspect it for themselves. The swarm only takes off once enough scouts are gathered at the same site. Moving is a one-way trip, so the colony verifies first.

What you're looking at: an illustration, not data. A swarm hangs from a branch while scouts fly between it and a hollow tree they're checking out.

Why this sticks. A swarm can't undo a bad move, and neither can you with Zelle or a wire. So do what the bees do: check the name, confirm new details by calling, send $1 first, and only then let the money fly.

06 Notes your style

The page you'd copy down.

  • Direct deposit: pay sent straight to your account. Give your routing number (9 digits, your bank) and account number (your account). Both are on the bottom of a check.
  • ACH: the batch network for direct deposit, autopays and bank transfers. Free, 1 to 3 business days (Monday to Friday, no holidays).
  • Wire transfer: same business day, about $25 to $35, almost impossible to reverse.
  • Peer-to-peer payment: Zelle (bank to bank, minutes, free) or Venmo and Cash App (app balance first). Instant transfer fee on Venmo: 1.75%, at least $0.25, at most $25.
  • Mobile deposit: first $275 of a check usually available the next business day, the rest by the second; longer for new accounts or over $6,725.
  • Cards can be disputed. Zelle, wires and friends-and-family payments are like cash. Purchase protection only on goods-and-services payments.
  • Rules: send fast money only to people you know, test with $1, check the name, call to confirm new bank details, start transfers 3 business days early.

09 Check

Exam-style questions.

Written the way a college exam would ask them. Each right answer on the first try is worth 10 xp.

On the job: you're the payroll specialist

You run payroll at a small company. A new hire, Dana, emails: "Please switch my direct deposit to my new account, routing 099100002, account 7781200034." It's the first time Dana has emailed you, and the email came from a personal address you've never seen.

Your task: decide what you do before changing anything, and explain why to your manager.

See a model answer

Don't change it from the email. Requests to switch direct deposit are a common scam: someone pretends to be an employee and redirects their paycheck. Once an ACH deposit lands in the wrong account, getting it back is hard.

Verify another way: call or talk to Dana in person, using contact details already in your records, not the ones in the email. Ask Dana to enter the change in the payroll system or sign the company's direct deposit form.

Then send a small test or wait one cycle: many payroll systems send a prenote, a $0 test, or keep a paper check for one pay period while the new account is confirmed.

Why it matters: it's the same rule as for any money you send. Check twice, confirm new details by calling, and only then let it move.
